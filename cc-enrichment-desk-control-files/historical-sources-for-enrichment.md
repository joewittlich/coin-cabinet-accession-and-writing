# Historical Information Sources for CC Companion Enrichment

Free or openly accessible internet resources useful for ruler lives, authority context, mint and struck-year significance, iconography, economics, Christian history, and related background. These complement the locked catalog corpora (RIC, Sear, DOC, RPC, etc.).

In any `enrichment/CC-00xx.json`, list only works actually opened, in the companion skill order:

1. Catalog corpus for the series  
2. Matching type line in the live online corpus  
3. Named primary source only if a sentence depends on it  
4. Wikipedia page title for the person whose life is sketched  

---

## Primary Sources and Near-Period Historians (Translations)

- **[Perseus Digital Library](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/)** / [beta](https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/)  
  Large collection of Greek and Latin texts with English translations (Herodotus, Thucydides, Polybius, Livy, Tacitus, Suetonius, Cassius Dio, Plutarch, etc.).

- **[LacusCurtius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/)**  
  High-quality public-domain texts and translations: Suetonius, Cassius Dio, Tacitus, Livy, Ammianus Marcellinus, and many others. Strong for imperial biography.

- **[Internet History Sourcebooks Project](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/)**  
  Ancient History Sourcebook, Medieval Sourcebook, and Byzantine sections. Curated short extracts and full texts (Fordham / Paul Halsall). Excellent starting point for both Roman and Byzantine.

- **[Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/)** and **[Internet Archive](https://archive.org/)**  
  Many public-domain translations of Livy, Suetonius, Dio, Ammianus, Procopius, Anna Komnene, etc. Search by author.

- **[Livius.org](https://www.livius.org/)**  
  Reliable modern summaries and selected primary excerpts on Roman and Hellenistic topics, with good archaeological and site context.

## Ruler Biographies and Encyclopedias

- **De Imperatoribus Romanis (DIR)**  
  Peer-reviewed biographical essays on Roman and Byzantine emperors (Augustus to Constantine XI). Currently best accessed via [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/). Includes stemmata and battle notes.  
  (Original site: roman-emperors.org — check current hosting status.)

- **[Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/)** (specific emperor or ruler pages)  
  Allowed in enrichment `sources` for the person whose life is sketched. Use the page title form given in the skill.

- **Oxford Classical Dictionary** and related Brill/Oxford reference works  
  Concise, authoritative entries on persons, offices, and institutions (many available via library access or partial open versions).

- **[Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/)** and **Oxford Reference** short entries  
  Quick orientation on rulers and events when a fuller primary source is not needed.

## Classical Culture, Religion, and Iconography

- **[Perseus Art & Archaeology Artifact Browser](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifactBrowser)**  
  Images and descriptions of sculptures, vases, coins, and sites that illustrate reverse types and symbols.

- **[Livius.org](https://www.livius.org/)** articles on gods, myths, and civic symbols  
  Clear explanations of Athena, Zeus, Herakles, Roma, Victoria, etc., and how they appear on coinage.

- **[Theoi Greek Mythology](https://www.theoi.com/)**  
  Systematic treatment of deities, attributes, and myths useful for Greek and early Roman reverse types.

- **[LacusCurtius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/)** secondary works (e.g., Smith’s *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Antiquities*)  
  Public-domain reference for objects, cults, and institutions that appear on coins.

- Internet Archive scans of classic works such as Agnes Baldwin Brett, *Symbolism on Greek Coins*, and similar older iconographic studies.

## Economics, Monetary Systems, and Trade

- **[OpenStax World History](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/7-3-the-roman-economy-trade-taxes-and-conquest)**  
  Free textbook chapters on the Roman economy, trade, and taxes.

- Scholarly overviews available via open repositories or university sites on Roman monetary history (Howgego, Crawford, Harris, Temin summaries). Search for “Roman money supply,” “denarius debasement,” or “Pax Romana trade.”

- **Coin Hoards of the Roman Republic Online** and related ANS/OCRE materials  
  Useful for understanding circulation and economic reach when a type or mint is discussed.

- Medieval economic context: Internet Medieval Sourcebook sections on economic life, and open articles on Carolingian, Italian communal, and Islamic monetary systems.

## Christian and Religious History (Especially Byzantine)

- **[Internet Medieval Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/)** (Byzantium and Religion sections)  
  Extracts from church councils, hagiography, and imperial religious policy.

- **[New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/)**  
  Catholic Encyclopedia and many patristic texts; useful for early Christian and Byzantine ecclesiastical context.

- **Fordham Byzantine Studies page** and related sourcebook sections  
  Iconoclasm, Christology debates, and imperial church relations.

- Primary Christian historians (Eusebius, Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret) available via Perseus, LacusCurtius, or Sourcebooks.

## Byzantine Studies

- **[Library of Congress Byzantine Empire Research Guide](https://guides.loc.gov/byzantine-empire/electronic-resources)**  
  Links to primary sources, digitized manuscripts, and reference works.

- **[Internet Medieval Sourcebook – Byzantium](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/)**  
  Anna Komnene, Procopius, chronicles, and legal extracts.

- **[Princeton Byzantine Studies databases](https://library.princeton.edu/byzantine/)**  
  Translations and manuscript lists.

- Open translations of key Byzantine historians (Procopius, Michael Psellos, Niketas Choniates, Anna Komnene) via Internet Archive or university sites.

---

## Medieval European and Islamic History

- **[Internet Medieval Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/)**  
  Full range of European, Crusader, and early Islamic texts.

- **[Online Medieval Sources Bibliography](https://medievaldigital.ace.fordham.edu/sourcebooks-and-bibliographies/)** (Fordham)  
  Searchable database of available online or printed medieval texts.

- Islamic series: open resources on Umayyad/Abbasid coinage and history (Album concordance is the catalog standard; historical context via Sourcebook Islamic sections and university open courses).

- Regional notes for Hungarian, Venetian, and other medieval issues in the catalog: national historical institutes or open encyclopedia entries when a specific ruler or mint is involved.

## Geography, Sites, Archaeology, and Mint Context

- **[Livius.org](https://www.livius.org/)** site articles  
  Ancient cities with modern locations (useful for mint overviews).

- **[Vici.org](https://vici.org/)** or similar archaeological atlases  
  Site locations.

- **[Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/)**  
  Ancient place names, coordinates, and bibliography (strong for mint geography).

- Museum collection databases (British Museum, Metropolitan Museum, ANS)  
  Object records and historical notes on sites and types.

## General Academic Gateways and Reference

- **[Internet History Sourcebooks Project](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/)** (main portal)  
  Best single starting point across periods.

- **[Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/)** and **[LacusCurtius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/)**  
  Primary for classical.

- University open courseware and syllabi (search “Roman history open course” or “Byzantine history sources”) for curated reading lists.

- **[Internet Archive](https://archive.org/)**  
  Scanned older monographs and translations that are otherwise hard to obtain.

---

## Practical Note for the Workflow

These resources support the facts pass (accurate life events, mint role, type meaning, economic or religious context) and the voice pass (clear spoken English). Prefer primary or high-quality secondary sources over popular summaries when a specific claim appears in the enrichment. Keep the JSON `sources` list short and limited to works actually consulted for that coin.
